Mobility · UX case study

Malta Taxi — booking a ride in a place you don't know.

A taxi booking app for a country most of its riders are visiting for the first time — built around knowing the price before you commit, watching the car actually arrive, and a booking flow short enough to finish on a kerb.

Role
UI/UX Designer
Timeline
Jan – Jul 2024
Platform
iOS & Android

01 — Project overview

An app that has to earn trust from someone with no local knowledge to check it against.

Malta's riders are disproportionately tourists: people who don't know the street names, don't know what a fair fare looks like, and have no way to tell a reliable driver from a chancer. Every one of the design decisions below comes back to that.

The problem
Riders struggle to book reliable taxis quickly, with no clear pricing and no real-time tracking.
Users
Riders in Malta — most of them tourists on a first visit.
My role
UI/UX designer — mobile app design for iOS and Android.
Outcome
A complete booking app, from onboarding to tour exploration.

02 — The challenge

Three gaps, one broken first ride.

Users in Malta, especially tourists, face difficulty booking reliable taxis quickly. The lack of clear pricing, real-time tracking, and a simple booking flow creates confusion and reduces trust.

They are not three separate complaints — so the fix could not be three features bolted on separately. It had to be one sequence in which each answer arrives before the doubt it settles.

01

No clear pricing

Not knowing the price is what makes the wait feel risky.

02

No real-time tracking

Not being able to see the car is what makes the price feel like a gamble.

03

A slow booking flow

A flow that takes too long gives both doubts the time they need to turn into a cancelled ride.

03 — User needs

What riders actually asked for.

The five needs the design was held against, in the deck's own words, with the design consequence underneath each.

01

Quick and hassle-free taxi booking

The booking flow is the product. Anything that isn't needed to get a car moving was pushed out of it.

02

Clear pricing before confirming rides

The fare is shown while the ride is still cancellable, not after. Trust is decided in that gap.

03

Real-time tracking for safety and reliability

A live map with the nearby taxis on it, so the wait is something you can watch rather than endure.

04

Easy navigation for unfamiliar locations

Destination search that works for someone who can't spell the street they want — the tourist case, treated as the default.

05

Simple and guided user experience

Onboarding sets location up front, so the first screen after it is already useful instead of asking for a permission mid-task.

04 — The solution

Four moves, in the order you meet them.

A taxi booking app that takes the guesswork out of the ride — sequenced the way a rider actually hits each answer, rather than listed by feature.

01

Quick onboarding and location setup

Location is asked for once, up front, with the reason attached — so no later screen has to stop and ask mid-task.

02

Real-time map with nearby taxis

The wait becomes visible. Cars on a map answer "is one actually coming?" before the question is asked.

03

Easy destination search and booking

Search built for someone who can't spell the street, and a confirmation step that shows the fare while cancelling is still free.

04

Tour exploration for travellers

Treats a tourist as a tourist rather than a commuter who happens to be lost — places to go, not just a field to fill.

01

Onboarding & location setup

Two screens before anything is booked, both answering the fifth need: the app should be useful the moment onboarding ends.

Onboarding
States the promise and offers one action.
Location setup
Asks for the permission with the reason attached — "set your location to start exploring" — rather than as a bare system prompt.

05 — Visual language

The project palette.

Read off the screens rather than chosen next to them: the app render was decoded and its opaque pixels counted, so these five are the artwork's actual palette in order of area.

Malta red#FA0501

The brand. Carries the onboarding screen, the primary action and the map pin.

Paper#FFFFFF

The larger half of the app. Red is the accent; white is the room it works in.

Red tint#FF5C51

The soft halo behind the map card — the brand red at lower weight, for depth.

Map mint#B9F4C8

The only cool colour, with one job: it is the map, so the map is never mistaken for a card.

Ink#000000

Type and iconography. Pure black, because everything it sits on is white or mint.
